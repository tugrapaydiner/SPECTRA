## Prospective contract

Five unopened WAP-A optical-conflict graphs were fixed before acquisition: `wap02a`, `wap03a`, `wap04a`, `wap07a`, and `wap08a`. Each graph supplied one independently clustered problem. The frozen workload generated 1,024 distinct satisfiable eight-vertex support restrictions per graph. Seven arms were run in forward, reverse, and one fixed shuffled order, for 105 complete sessions and 107,520 durable full answers.

Every session charged immutable case decoding and digest validation, fresh formula/index and solver construction, all 1,024 queries, complete answer materialisation, an independently owned original graph/list/query check for every answer, diagnostics, and disposal. Process launch/import was retained as a separate cold-wall receipt. Case construction, source build, evidence compression/transport, and optional independent compiler-certificate audit were separately disclosed.

Primary candidate: exact implication-SCC quotient (`scc`). Primary control: persistent native MiniCard with one Boolean per original lightpath, compact binary-list encoding, and learned-clause retention across all queries.

Frozen pass conditions:

1. all 105 sessions and all 107,520 answers audit successfully;
2. upper exact graph-clustered 95% mean ratio `SCC / MiniCard <= 0.50`;
3. upper exact graph-clustered 95% p95 ratio `<= 1.10`;
4. SCC has lower mean complete-session cost on every holdout graph.

## What the result establishes

The evidence establishes a large, reproducible complete-pipeline advantage for repeated support queries on this fixed WAP optical-conflict benchmark family under the frozen synthetic two-colour-list/query contract. It survives a strong persistent native cardinality solver, a persistent CaDiCaL control, fixed query-order changes, complete output materialisation, original-input checking, and whole-graph clustered uncertainty.

## What it does not establish

- ordinary chromatic-number superiority;
- deployed wavelength-availability traces (lists and restrictions are synthetic);
- general SAT, list-colouring, or one-query superiority;
- algorithmic novelty from SCC equivalence substitution alone;
- an asymptotic improvement;
- ordinary-laptop or non-Linux portability;
- independent-team replication or external adoption.

The three development graphs and five holdouts belong to one benchmark family. The exact interval describes this fixed family; it is not a universal production SLO. Post-open reruns are reproduction only and cannot become a second confirmation study.

## Verdict

This passes the project's demanding **important-result** gate: a predeclared, large complete-cost effect against a serious practical competitor on genuinely unopened problem instances, with full witnesses, failure retention, exact graph-level inference, and an independently auditable compiler relation.

It is appropriate to present this as SPECTRA's flagship **research result**, provided the claim remains attached to the exact repeated-support-query contract above. The wider SPECTRA system is not thereby proven generally superior. Independent-team reproduction and an external user remain the next impact gates.
